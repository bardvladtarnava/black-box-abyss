# BLACK BOX — Hydrophone edition

A rescue pilot descends into Pelagic Station after hearing their own callsign on a twelve-year-old recorder. A lantern drone guards the flood lock. A piston crab occupies the pump cathedral, and a spindle eel nests in the tape archive. The Listener guards the master tape at 1,200 m.

**Rigs.** Both start with 28 hull and two cells. Rivet can anchor twice per encounter to absorb an incoming pulse. Echo spends a cell to reflect the contact's announced pulse. A cell can also repair up to 12 hull. Recovered cells and remaining hull carry forward. Every cleared non-final sector awards one cell and a choice of an extra cell or up to 8 hull.

**Hydrophone.** Each cutter hit (up to 5 damage) adds one noise mark. At two marks the pilot can match the contact's three-note signature to discharge a 4-point resonance hit and cancel its response. The signature is audible and visible. There is no timer. Against a contact with 4 hull this ends the fight. Against an unarmored contact with 5 hull, the cutter ends the fight. A surviving contact responding to a third cutter hit deals 3 bonus damage and resets noise. Both rig abilities clear noise.

**Sector tactics.** The flood lock teaches noise. The crab seals its shell on a strong pulse. The eel punishes accumulated noise. The Listener shields itself at half hull. Breach deals 7 damage and disables armor for one sector, with a single use before reloading. Brace clears noise and absorbs up to 4 damage. The action bar previews availability and effects, with shortcuts or arrows and Enter.

**Turns.** The pilot acts first. Scan, listening, editing a phrase, invalid input and unavailable actions are free. A successful action spends the turn. A destroyed contact has no response. Leaving ends the expedition. Zero hull loses it. Recovering the final tape wins it.

**Relationships.** Character enforces hull bounds. Hero adds an independently owned Inventory of Cell objects. Rivet and Echo implement the same ability method. Dive resolves commands; an accepted turn produces events. The terminal animates and scores those events after resolution, so music latency, frame rate and keyboard speed cannot affect damage.
